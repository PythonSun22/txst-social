The Vision

Campus already has structure.
Most social networks ignore it.

Boko Lynx is a web platform for the Texas State community that organizes people and content around the university's colleges — so a student's feed reflects where they actually study, live, and belong.

Conventional where it counts

Profiles, a feed, posts, comments and reactions — the interactions students already understand, built to work reliably.

Distinctive where it matters

An animated, interactive college-navigation experience gives the application an identity a generic CRUD app does not have.

Engineered to be split

A clean frontend, backend and database separation that six developers can work on in parallel without blocking each other.

Features — What Makes It Boko Lynx

The parts a generic social app does not have

  • College communities. Texas State's colleges are the platform's major organizational unit. Students affiliate with one, browse its page, see its posts, and discover others studying alongside them.
  • Groups. Smaller communities formed around interests, subjects, organizations, events and hobbies — scoped to whatever development time allows.
  • Search & discovery. A direct way to find users, colleges and groups. Deliberately simple: recommendation systems are out of scope.
  • An animated interface. Animated backgrounds, smooth page transitions, scroll-driven effects and parallax, built with GSAP where it earns its place.
  • Interactive college navigation. A polished crest-and-banner GUI for moving between colleges — the single most distinctive surface in the product.
  • ML content moderation. A small-scale classifier flagging content for review, kept intentionally modest in ambition.

Animation enhances the interface. Where it would interfere with accessibility or navigation, the interface wins.

How We Work

The agreements that keep six people in sync

  • No direct commits to main. Feature branches, pull requests, one approving review from someone who did not write the code.
  • One weekly meeting of 45 minutes, plus a written midweek check-in: what shipped, what is next, what is blocked.
  • Reviews are due within 24 hours. A stalled review blocks a teammate and counts as a missed commitment.
  • Definition of done: it works, it has a test, it is reviewed and merged, and it runs on the deployed environment — not only on the author's machine.
  • Blocked for a day means asking. Silent blockage turns a one-hour problem into a lost week.
  • Scope changes go to the whole team. A new feature enters only by displacing something already planned.

Risks & Open Questions

What we already know could go wrong

Highest risk

The ML moderation system. It is the only in-scope item requiring a skill the rest of the project does not. Scheduled last, deliberately, so that cutting it costs nothing else.

Open question

Follows, or communities only? Follows and college communities are two different answers to “what fills my feed.” Building both means neither is fully realized. To be decided before Week 9.

Schedule

Animation competing with function. Visual work is assigned to a dedicated owner and runs in parallel, so a polished interface never arrives at the cost of a working feed.

Coordination

Six people on one codebase. Vertical slices and an agreed API contract mean parallel work; without them, six developers produce less than three.
